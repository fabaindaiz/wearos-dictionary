# Proposal: readout-per-silent-outcome

A filter that prints nothing is not a verdict. An empty output can mean no match, a failed command, a wrong pattern or a target that matched itself, so the step that follows must read the exit code, not the silence, and a result is never read from the last line of a pipeline, since the pipeline's status is the last stage's, not the gate's. A gate chained into a commit must keep its own status visible to the chain, and must not be sent to the background where its failure is out of sight.

## Evidence

One carrier, one owner, with the same slip also seen once in a second carrier of that owner. At least four occurrences, counted from the session log: a search for a verifier's verdict matched nothing and was read as an empty result until the exit code was read; a count that failed after about ten minutes of work because an empty result had been taken as success; a gate piped into a tail reported success over a failed build, twice, and once more as eight printed failures with a zero status; and a chain of gate, commit and build with a trailing background marker sent the gate and the commit to the background, where the commit failed out of sight. Each was caught by re-reading the status, not by the filter. Literature: the candidate's own; no new source consulted.
